Open Robotics has a survey asking for _your_ feedback. Fill it in to enter a prize draw!

[Open Robotics](https://www.openrobotics.org/) works to support the development, distribution, and adoption of open-source software for use in robotics research, education, and product development. As an organisation and as a movement, we know a lot about the software, tools and standards that we build, but _we do not know enough about the people who use them_ in their work, in their hobbies, and in their studies.

What motivates people to use the Open Robotics Suite (ROS, ros-controls, Space ROS, Gazebo, Open-RMF, and the infrastructure that supports them), in whole or in part? What motivates companies to make their products depend on our software? What features are actually relied upon every day, and which are no longer necessary? Or, conversely, what motivates someone to _not_ use the Open Robotics Suite? To begin answering these questions, we are starting a new annual survey of everyone who is or could be using our software.

We are asking anyone and everyone who is in robotics, in any way, to answer this survey. Even if you do not use any Open Robotics software, or you think that ROS is not suitable for your work, or you’ve only just started learning Gazebo, or you tried and have abandoned ROS, we still want to hear from you. If you’ve never used ROS, Gazebo, or any of our other software, we want to hear from you!

Achieving our mission requires knowing who we are or should be building software for, no matter who they are or where they use it. This survey is how we get to know our community better.

**You can fill in the survey by following this link**: [https://go.openrobotics.org/orsurvey26](https://go.openrobotics.org/orsurvey26)

### [](https://discourse.ros.org/t/how-are-commercial-proprietary-ros-2-components-typically-distributed/58581#p-118556-prize-draw-1)Prize draw

All survey participants will have the opportunity to enter a prize draw to win one item of their choice from the [Open Robotics Store](https://store.openrobotics.org/). At the end of the survey you will have the opportunity to enter your email address for the purposes of entering this prize draw. You must enter a valid email address to be eligible for the prize draw. **50 participants will be chosen at random** from those who enter.

### [](https://discourse.ros.org/t/how-are-commercial-proprietary-ros-2-components-typically-distributed/58581#p-118556-deadline-2)Deadline

**The survey period ends on the 30th of November, 2026**. All _completed_ surveys received by this date will be eligible for the prize draw.

### [](https://discourse.ros.org/t/how-are-commercial-proprietary-ros-2-components-typically-distributed/58581#p-118556-who-should-fill-in-the-survey-3)Who should fill in the survey?

_Everyone_! We want the opinions of everyone working in, studying in, and playing in robotics. We want the opinions of every individual - not just one response per company. Get your colleagues to fill it in, your team mates, your staff, your classmates. The more information we have, the better we can work to ensure that the software you depend on better meets your needs - or, if you don’t use it, gets to the point where you want to!

I’d like to understand the common deployment patterns for commercial ROS 2 software.

My background is in mobile/on-device AI. In the mobile ecosystem, binary distribution has fairly clear boundaries: an SDK vendor might provide an AAR for Android or an XCFramework for iOS, while keeping the underlying implementation proprietary.

As we move into robotics, the boundaries seem less standardized.

For example, consider an optimized AI inference runtime targeting Jetson or another Linux-based robotics platform. The core runtime is proprietary, but customers need to integrate it into an existing ROS 2 system.

One possible architecture is:

```plaintext
Proprietary inference runtime
        |
        +-- C/C++ API
        |
        +-- ROS 2 adapter
                |
                +-- node
                +-- launch files
                +-- configuration
```

The actual distribution could then happen through Debian packages, containers, prebuilt binaries, or potentially ROS packages themselves.

I’m curious how companies shipping production robotics systems approach this in practice.

Do you generally treat ROS 2 as the distribution boundary itself, or as an integration layer on top of independently packaged software?

And has the ROS ecosystem developed any common best practices around binary-only commercial components, versioning, dependency management, and deployment?

I’m particularly interested in experiences from people who have shipped ROS 2 software to customer robots rather than only deploying software internally.

[Transitive](https://github.com/transitiverobotics/transitive), the open-source platform for building full-stack robotics capabilities we’ve built, is one such distribution channel. Each capability contains one or more of:

*   a robot component (which is often a ROS node)
*   a cloud component, and
*   one or more web components.

While all our existing capabilities combine all three, you could use it to distribute a robot-only capability as well. Capabilities are npm packages, i.e., tar-balls with dependencies and (pre/post-)install script(s), so they can carry any payload, including pre-compiled binaries.

If you want to explore this, I recommend you first try it out as a user on our [hosted offering](https://transitiverobotics.com/) (accounts are free and several capabilities are, too), and then read these sections:

*   [What is Transitive? | Transitive Robotics](https://transitiverobotics.com/docs/learn/intro/)
*   [Develop: Introduction](https://transitiverobotics.com/docs/develop/introduction/)
