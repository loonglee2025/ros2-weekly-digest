Open Robotics has a survey asking for _your_ feedback. Fill it in to enter a prize draw!

[Open Robotics](https://www.openrobotics.org/) works to support the development, distribution, and adoption of open-source software for use in robotics research, education, and product development. As an organisation and as a movement, we know a lot about the software, tools and standards that we build, but _we do not know enough about the people who use them_ in their work, in their hobbies, and in their studies.

What motivates people to use the Open Robotics Suite (ROS, ros-controls, Space ROS, Gazebo, Open-RMF, and the infrastructure that supports them), in whole or in part? What motivates companies to make their products depend on our software? What features are actually relied upon every day, and which are no longer necessary? Or, conversely, what motivates someone to _not_ use the Open Robotics Suite? To begin answering these questions, we are starting a new annual survey of everyone who is or could be using our software.

We are asking anyone and everyone who is in robotics, in any way, to answer this survey. Even if you do not use any Open Robotics software, or you think that ROS is not suitable for your work, or you’ve only just started learning Gazebo, or you tried and have abandoned ROS, we still want to hear from you. If you’ve never used ROS, Gazebo, or any of our other software, we want to hear from you!

Achieving our mission requires knowing who we are or should be building software for, no matter who they are or where they use it. This survey is how we get to know our community better.

**You can fill in the survey by following this link**: [https://go.openrobotics.org/orsurvey26](https://go.openrobotics.org/orsurvey26)

All survey participants will have the opportunity to enter a prize draw to win one item of their choice from the [Open Robotics Store](https://store.openrobotics.org/). Since it was born during the 2020 pandemic, [PlanSys2](https://plansys2.github.io/) has been the basis of papers, master’s theses, master courses, and PhD theses. As far as I know, some companies use it to control their logistics robots, and it has even been to space on board the Astrobee robots, as I was told at ROSCon 2022 in Kyoto. Last year I had the chance to present it at ROSCon 2025 in Singapore:

![Image 8: PlanSys2, the ROS 2 Planning System Past, present and future](https://us1.discourse-cdn.com/flex022/uploads/ros/original/3X/4/4/44ad5e7fd76b1e105cf2c2300b60b71ca728a199.jpeg)

[PlanSys2, the ROS 2 Planning System Past, present and future](https://vimeo.com/1136377384 "PlanSys2, the ROS 2 Planning System Past, present and future")

PDDL is as relevant as ever: it is a formal language for task planning, and PlanSys2 has become an essential baseline for much of the current work on task planning with LLMs.

Today I am happy to announce **PlanSys2 4.0.0**, a release that marks the technical maturity of this PDDL planning and execution framework. Two things define it:

*   **Reliability and robustness.** PlanSys2 now copes with any start order, processes that die and come back (they restart and recover their state), lost messages, network delays, loaded systems, API misuse and hostile inputs. It was tested with sanitizers, fuzzing and a stress bench across Fast DDS, Cyclone DDS and Zenoh.
*   **PDDL coverage, from PDDL 1.2 to PDDL+**, both in the domains it loads and in the execution of their plans: from STRIPS and typing to conditional effects, derived predicates, numeric fluents, durative actions, timed initial literals, PDDL3 preferences and constraints, object fluents, and PDDL+ processes and events. The new STN behavior tree builder, now the default, executes plans faithfully: it keeps the concurrency and the time constraints between their actions. This example ([domain](https://github.com/PlanSys2/ros2_planning_system_examples/blob/rolling/plansys2_pddlplus_example/pddl/greenhouse.pddl) and [problem](https://github.com/PlanSys2/ros2_planning_system_examples/blob/rolling/plansys2_pddlplus_example/pddl/watering.pddl)) shows what PlanSys2 now plans and executes.

This is possible thanks to [Kairos](https://github.com/PlanSys2/kairos), now the default planner of PlanSys2. As far as we know, it covers [more of PDDL](https://github.com/PlanSys2/kairos/blob/main/docs/pddl-support.md) than any other planner, and [faster](https://github.com/PlanSys2/kairos/blob/main/docs/benchmark.md).

PlanSys2 4.0.0 is a single version released for **Humble, Jazzy, Kilted and Lyrical**, and it will be available in the next sync of each distribution.

Feedback, issues and contributions are very welcome at [github.com/PlanSys2](https://github.com/PlanSys2/ros2_planning_system).
